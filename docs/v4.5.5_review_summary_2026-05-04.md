## 4. Three top headline findings from Round 3

### Finding 1 — The 50s decade may concentrate the ADRB3-fibrosis signal (v4.5.5 Slide 29; ROUND-4-HEDGED)

| Older-group definition | ADRB2 × fibrosis (subq F) | ADRB3 × fibrosis (subq F) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 50-79 (50s included) | **r = −0.43**, p<0.001 | r = −0.17, NS |
| 60-79 (50s excluded) | **r = −0.48**, p<0.001 | r = −0.06, NS |
| 50-59 only (peri window) | r = −0.37, p<0.01 | **r = −0.36**, p<0.01 |

Round-4 Fisher-z tests for cross-stratification differences:
- ADRB3 broad-incl (-0.17) vs broad-excl (-0.06): **p = 0.43, NS**
- ADRB3 peri (-0.36) vs broad-excl (-0.06): **p = 0.054, marginally sub-threshold**

**Interpretation (Round-4-hedged)**: ADRB2's fibrosis effect is numerically robust across age slices and intensifies when 50s are removed. For ADRB3, the directional pattern is suggestive of an effect concentrated in the 50s decade, but neither cross-stratification comparison clears the conventional α=0.05 threshold. The "ADRB3 vanishes" framing of v4.5.4 was not supported by the Fisher-z tests. The hedged headline is: *the data are consistent with ADRB3 impairment being acute-and-transient (peaking during the perimenopausal transition) and ADRB2 impairment being lasting, but a larger cohort is needed to formally establish this*.

### Finding 2 — ADRB2 vs ADRB3 specialization on the fibrosis axis is statistically robust; the other axes are directional only (v4.5.5 Slide 43; ROUND-4-HEDGED)

In female subq (n=233), Williams' tests for dependent correlations (using r_ADRB2_ADRB3 = +0.287 from the same samples):

| Endpoint | ADRB2 R² | ADRB3 R² | Williams t | p | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Age | 0.021 | 0.047 | +0.96 | 0.34 | ns |
| CCL2 | 0.090 | 0.027 | -1.82 | 0.069 | ns |
| **Fibrosis** | **0.174** | **0.026** | **-3.54** | **0.0004** | **\*\*\*** |
| Senescence | 0.076 | 0.093 | +0.41 | 0.68 | ns |

**Interpretation (Round-4-hedged)**: only the **fibrosis** axis shows a statistically significant difference between ADRB2 and ADRB3 in female subq, with ADRB2 dominant (p=0.0004). The numerical Age, CCL2, and Senescence differences are directionally consistent with receptor specialization but are not statistically distinguishable at n=233 (p>0.06 for all three). The two receptors are not redundant on the fibrosis axis (formally established). The other apparent specializations are descriptive observations needing larger N. Lead manuscript framing on the fibrosis specialization; treat the others as exploratory.

### Finding 3 — Score-based stratification differs from age-bin stratification (v4.5.4 Slide 47)

The senMayoLike v2 transcriptional score divides females into tertiles that **partially but imperfectly align** with age-bin pre/peri/post grouping. The percentage of "pre" samples by age that fall into the high-tertile (T3, "more post-like") is **15.4% in subcutaneous** (12 of 78 pre samples) and **29.2% in visceral** (21 of 72). Cohen's kappa between the age-bin and score-tertile classifications is **0.169 in subcutaneous and 0.100 in visceral** — both fall in the "slight agreement" range (Landis & Koch 1977: <0.20 = slight; 0.21-0.40 = fair). The two groupings are largely discordant, supporting the use of score-based grouping as a sensitivity analysis. For the manuscript:

- **Primary stratification**: age-bin pre/peri/post (existing, conventional)
- **Sensitivity stratification**: senMayoLike score tertiles (new in v4.5.4)
- **Concordance**: report agreement and document the discordant samples

The score-based approach also enables stratification within an age band: e.g., 40-49 women can be split by score tertile to reveal whether the apparent peri-menopausal effects are uniformly distributed or concentrated in transcriptionally-advanced women within the band.
